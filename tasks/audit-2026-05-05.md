mean OOD delta is negative.

This matters for the WHO-asymmetry story: row spread is dominated by
which specialists were trained on the cleanest distributions. If law
and physics are mostly format-matchers, the row variance partly reflects
training-data quality, not "intrinsic primary-domain difficulty under
collaboration." A defensible paper version of the claim must condition
on a *uniform* verification standard or report the result with and
without the marginal specialists.

### Matched-solo-accuracy selection

This is the experimental control that lets us claim "LoRA and FT at
the *same* solo accuracy collaborate differently." We do not yet have
a script that:

1. Loads each `checkpoint-{1500,3000,4500,6000}` and the corresponding
   LoRA adapter.
2. Runs the *same* 50-mixed-MMLU 3-CoT-round solo protocol used by
   the LoRA pair-grid.
3. Reports which FT checkpoint matches the LoRA solo accuracy within
   a tolerance (±2 pp seems reasonable).
4. Picks that FT checkpoint as the comparison anchor.

Without this, any LoRA-vs-FT delta we report can be attributed to
"the FT specialist was better trained" (or worse trained). Building
this script is the highest-impact concrete next task.

### 6a. NEW finding from this audit: C2W:W2C count ratio is base-rate-confounded

Re-running the per-cell C2W/W2C numbers from
`matrix_results.json` (data already on disk; see
`/tmp/halulujah_audit/conditional_rates.json`):

| Setting | Pooled C2W count | Pooled W2C count | Pooled C2W:W2C count ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.7B verified LoRA (5×5, 25 cells) | 82 | 625 | **0.13** |
| 4B Full FT (3 cells, +base helper) | 65 | 37 | **1.76** |

The raw count ratio at 1.7B LoRA is now *0.13* (W2C dominates), and
at 4B FT is *1.76* (C2W dominates). The earlier headline "3.4×
harmful switching invariant across protocols" (obj-007) was on the
PACE-pre-RP corpus where solo accuracy was high; with high solo,
there are many more "started correct" candidates than "started
wrong" candidates, so the count ratio is mechanically inflated.

When you normalize by the available pool (probability of switching
*conditioned on* starting correct vs starting wrong), the picture
flips:

| Setting | C2W given started-correct | W2C given started-wrong | Rate ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.7B verified LoRA (mean over 30 cells) | 32.7% | 46.7% | 0.70 |
| 4B Full FT (3 cells) | 14.7% | 23.3% | 0.63 |

Both rate ratios are below 1.0, meaning the *agent is more likely to
flip a wrong answer to a correct one than vice versa*, i.e.
collaboration is net-helpful in the rank-normalized sense, in
*both* LoRA and Full FT. The two settings differ in the *absolute*
flip rates: LoRA flips more often (32.7% / 46.7%) than FT (14.7% /
23.3%), which matches "LoRA is more sycophantic / less anchored,"
but the *direction* of asymmetry (always flipping to the helper's
answer regardless of correctness) is the same in both.

**Implication for the paper.** The headline "rank-constrained
adaptation destroys collaboration via C2W asymmetry" is not
defensible from the verified data when you control for the base-rate
artifact. The defensible claim is:

> *LoRA specialists update on peer answers more eagerly than
> Full-FT specialists at every solo-accuracy level we measured.
> The direction of switching (more flips toward correct than away,
> in both training regimes) is preserved by rank-constrained
> adaptation; only the magnitude is amplified.*

This is a smaller but cleaner claim. It is also consistent with
the intruder-dimension citation (Shuttleworth 2410.21228): rank
constraint introduces directions orthogonal to the pretrained
subspace, and those directions appear to amplify *all* updating,
not just harmful updating.

The audit recommends that **before any further paper writing**:

- The accuracy-delta claim and the C2W:W2C claim be presented as
  separate, with the latter explicitly framed as conditional rates
  rather than count ratios.
- The 1.7B FT pair-grid be run, so that the LoRA-vs-FT magnitude
  claim has a *paired* baseline rather than a 4B FT to 1.7B LoRA
  cross-comparison.
- The "matched solo accuracy" controls be enforced, since the rate
  ratios are a function of solo accuracy: math/medicine/physics LoRA
  are at 4-14% pre_acc on the pair-grid eval, while 4B FT bio/law/math
  are at 87/61/72%. These two settings cannot be compared directly.

## 6b. WHO-asymmetry ratio: canonical aggregator + roster sensitivity

### Reconciled to the obj-040 4.47× number

The 2026-05-04 confusion (4.47× obj-040 vs 4.74× audit-raw) was a
choice of cell value. obj-040's `plot_pair_grid.py` uses
**delta cells** (`pair_acc - solo_primary_acc`); my initial audit
recompute used **raw pair accuracy**. Both are mathematically valid;
they give different ratios because raw accuracy mixes the primary's
solo competence into the row spread.

**Canonical choice** (locked 2026-05-05 via
`src/scripts/compute_who_asymmetry.py`, summary at
`results/verified_pair_grid_qwen3_1p7b/who_summary.json`):

> Cell value = `pair_acc - solo_primary_acc` (delta).
> Roster = full 5×5 specialists, helpers = `[base] + DOMAINS` (6 cols).
> Row mean = mean across helpers; col mean = mean across primaries.
> Ratio = row_spread / col_spread.

Reasoning: the WHO-asymmetry claim is "primary identity affects
*gain from collaboration*". Subtracting solo_primary removes
row-wise baseline competence so the row spread reflects gain-vs-helpers,
not absolute task accuracy. With raw cells the row spread also
absorbs the solo-accuracy variance (e.g., biology 38% vs physics 12%
solo on this eval).

### Headline value: **4.47×**

Verified: `python -m src.scripts.compute_who_asymmetry --write` →
row spread 34.0 pp, col spread 7.6 pp, ratio 4.474. Exactly matches
obj-040's commit-message numbers.

### Roster-sensitivity sweep (canonical aggregator)

| Roster | Cell | Helpers | Row spread | Col spread | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full 5×5 (canonical) | delta | + base | 34.0 pp | 7.6 pp | **4.47×** |
| Full 5×5 | delta | specialists only | 37.6 pp | 6.0 pp | 6.27× |
| 3×3 (math/bio/law)¹ | delta | + base | 32.5 pp | 10.0 pp | 3.25× |
| 3×3 (math/bio/law) | delta | specialists only | 35.3 pp | 10.0 pp | 3.53× |
| 4×4 minus law² | delta | + base | 30.5 pp | 10.0 pp | 3.05× |
| 4×4 minus law | delta | specialists only | 35.7 pp | 8.3 pp | 4.28× |

¹ Restricting to specialists where solo > base on the pair-grid eval.
² Excluding the law specialist that overfits CaseHOLD format.

**Range: 3.05× to 6.27×**, with the canonical headline at **4.47×**.

The most-deflating restrictions (4×4 minus law, 3×3) drop to ~3×;
removing the base helper (specialists only) inflates because the base
helper has the lowest col mean (17.2 pp) and removing it tightens the
helper distribution. The paper should:

1. Report **4.47×** as the canonical headline (full 5×5 + base helper, delta cells).
2. Cite the **3.05×–6.27×** range as the roster-sensitivity envelope in §4.
3. Note that the lower-bound 3.05× holds when the law CaseHOLD format-matcher is excluded.

(Earlier audit drafts proposed 3.85× as the conservative number under
the *raw-accuracy* aggregator on the 3×3 restricted roster. With the
canonical *delta* aggregator the same restriction gives 3.25×; this
is the new conservative lower bound to disclose.)
